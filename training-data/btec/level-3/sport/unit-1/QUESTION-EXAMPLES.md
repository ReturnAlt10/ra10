# Question and Mark Scheme Examples — BTEC Sport Unit 1

## SECTION A — Skeletal System

Q1 (3 marks — Label): Name the three bones labelled A, B, C on the diagram of the arm.
MS: A Humerus (1) B Ulna (1) C Radius (1) Accept phonetic spelling.

Q2 (1 mark — State): State one type of cartilaginous joint.
MS: Vertebrae/cervical/thoracic/lumbar (1) OR Ribs/sternum (1) 
    OR Pubic symphysis (1) Accept phonetic spelling. DNA sacrum/coccyx.

Q3 (2 marks — State): State two types of bone found in the skeleton.
MS: Long (1) Short (1) Flat (1) Irregular (1) Sesamoid (1) — any two.

Q4 (4 marks — Explain): Explain how the functions of long bones and short bones 
help Shantel when playing as a goalkeeper in netball.
MS: Long bones enable movement to take place (1) to enable Shantel to move 
    across the goal to make a save (1) Short bones absorb shock/weight bearing (1)
    reducing risk of injuries when landing/allows her to push up off the ground (1)

Q5 (3 marks — Explain): Explain the function of ligaments in sport.
MS: Ligaments connect bone to bone (1) they provide strength/stability/support 
    to the joint (1) by stopping unwanted movement/reducing risk of injury (1)

Q6 (6 marks — Analyse): Analyse how the skeletal system supports Steph 
during a 60-minute netball match.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
- Bones provide attachment points for muscles → movement
- Joints allow range of movement needed (ball and socket at shoulder/hip,
  hinge at knee/elbow, pivot at neck)
- Hyaline cartilage reduces friction at joints → repeated jumping/landing
- Skeleton protects vital organs during contact
- Ligaments provide stability at joints → prevents injuries during direction changes
- Impact: can sustain movement patterns throughout match, reduced injury risk

## SECTION B — Muscular System

Q7 (3 marks — Label): Label the muscles A, B, C on the posterior view diagram.
MS: A Trapezius (1) DNA Traps. B Deltoid posterior (1) C Latissimus dorsi (1) 
    DNA Lats. Accept phonetic spelling.

Q8 (3 marks — State): State three characteristics of Type I muscle fibres.
MS: Contract slowly (1) Resistant to fatigue (1) Small in size (1) Red in colour (1)
    High capillary density (1) High number of mitochondria (1) 
    High myoglobin stores (1) Oxidative (1) — any three.

Q9 (3 marks — Explain): Explain muscular hypertrophy and its effect on 
Marcellous as a sprinter.
MS: Increase in size/strength of muscles (1) enables him to generate 
    more power/force (1) and run a faster time/improve sprint performance (1)

Q10 (2 marks — Describe): Describe the all-or-none law.
MS: For a motor unit to contract (1) it must receive an impulse/signal (1)
    all muscle fibres contract or none contract (1) — any two.

Q11 (2 marks — Explain): Explain how increased muscle pliability following 
a warm up will benefit a tennis player.
MS: Increases the amount muscles can stretch/makes them more flexible (1)
    increases stride length/reduces risk of injury (1)

Q12 (6 marks — Analyse): Analyse the chronic muscular adaptations that would 
benefit Crystal following 6 months of endurance cycling training.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
- Increased myoglobin stores → more oxygen stored in muscles → more aerobic energy
- Increased number of mitochondria → more aerobic energy production
- Increased glycogen storage → sustain high intensity for longer in race
- Increased capillary density → more blood/oxygen delivered to muscle
- Increased muscle pliability → reduced injury risk, greater range of movement
- Hypertrophy of slow twitch fibres → more force for sustained effort
- Impact: Crystal can maintain higher power output, fatigue onset delayed,
  complete races in faster times

## SECTION C — Respiratory System

Q13 (4 marks — Describe): Describe the mechanism of inspiration during exercise.
MS: Diaphragm contracts/external intercostal muscles contract with more force (1)
    Additional muscles used (scalenes/SCM/pectoralis minor) (1)
    Volume of thoracic cavity increases/ribs move up and out more (1)
    Pressure in thoracic cavity reduced (1) More air comes in (1) — any four.

Q14 (2 marks — Describe): Describe the role of internal intercostal muscles 
when expiring during exercise.
MS: Contract (1) to bring ribs down and in/reduce volume of thoracic cavity 
    quicker (1)

Q15 (2 marks — Describe): Describe asthma and its effect on a sports performer.
MS: Airways become restricted (1) making it harder to breathe/wheezing/
    coughing/shortness of breath (1)

Q16 (4 marks — Explain): Explain how chemical control of breathing removes 
waste products during exercise.
MS: Chemoreceptors (1) detect increased levels of carbon dioxide/lower pH (1)
    send messages to the medulla oblongata (1) therefore breathing rate 
    increases to remove carbon dioxide (1)

Q17 (6 marks — Analyse): Nancy is a netball player. Analyse how her respiratory 
system will respond and adapt following a long-term training programme.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
Acute responses: breathing rate increases, tidal volume increases, 
minute ventilation increases (RR x TV), IRV decreases, ERV decreases
Chronic adaptations: increased vital capacity, stronger respiratory muscles,
increased diffusion rates, increased number/size of alveoli, 
increased tidal volume at rest
Impact: more oxygen available to muscles, higher intensity sustained,
fatigue delayed, can maintain movement around court in 4th quarter

## SECTION D — Cardiovascular System

Q18 (4 marks — Label): Label structures A, B, C, D on the diagram of the heart.
MS: A Pulmonary artery (1) B Left atrium (1) C Aortic/semi-lunar valve (1) 
    D Right ventricle (1) Accept phonetic spelling.

Q19 (2 marks — Describe): Describe the function of capillaries.
MS: Connect arteries and veins/arterioles and venules (1)
    provide site for gas exchange (1)

Q20 (1 mark — State): State the component of blood that carries most oxygen.
MS: Red blood cells/RBC (1)

Q21 (4 marks — Explain): Explain the impact of increased blood volume on Robin's 
performance in a tennis match.
MS: More blood increases ability to transfer more oxygen to the working muscle (1)
    and remove carbon dioxide (1) therefore enabling Robin to play at a higher 
    intensity for a longer duration (1) and delay the onset of fatigue/
    recover quicker between points (1)

Q22 (6 marks — Analyse): Analyse the functions of the cardiovascular system 
that support Robin during a 60-minute tennis match.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
- Delivering oxygen and nutrients to working muscles
- Increased aerobic energy production
- Removing waste products (CO2 and lactate) from tissue to avoid build-up
- Thermoregulation: vasodilation of blood vessels near skin → excess heat lost
- Vascular shunt: blood redistributed from non-essential organs to muscles
- Impact: Robin can work at higher intensity longer, fatigue later,
  still run around court and reach shots in final set

Q23 (6 marks — Analyse): Analyse the chronic cardiovascular adaptations 
following Callum's 3-month football training programme.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
- Cardiac hypertrophy: heart bigger, left ventricle wall thicker → 
  larger/more forceful contraction → SV increases
- Increased stroke volume → cardiac output increases → more O2 to muscles
- Decreased resting heart rate → heart doesn't work as hard at rest
- Capillarisation → more blood to working muscles → more efficient O2 delivery
- Increased blood volume and haemoglobin → more O2 carried
- Impact: fatigue later in match, maintain faster pace/higher intensity,
  can run down wing and recover

## SECTION E — Energy Systems

Q24 (3 marks — State): Name the three stages of the aerobic energy system in order.
MS: 1 Aerobic glycolysis (1) 2 Krebs cycle/citric acid cycle (1) 
    3 Electron transport chain/ETC (1) Accept phonetic spelling.

Q25 (3 marks — Explain): Explain why the ATP-PC energy system is used 
during the high jump.
MS: The high jump is a short explosive activity lasting under 10 seconds (1)
    Phosphocreatine breaks down to release energy (1) which is used to 
    resynthesize ATP without oxygen (1)

Q26 (4 marks — Explain): Explain two aerobic adaptations and how each 
benefits Rhea as a hockey player.
MS: Increased storage of glycogen (1) enabling Rhea to keep playing at 
    high intensity in latter stages of match (1)
    Increased number of mitochondria (1) increases ability to produce more 
    aerobic energy and last the duration of the game (1)

Q27 (4 marks — Describe): Describe how the lactate energy system provides 
energy during the 400m run.
MS: Glucose/glycogen broken down anaerobically via glycolysis (1)
    produces ATP (1) pyruvate converted to lactate when insufficient O2 (1)
    fuels medium-high intensity activity lasting 10 seconds to 3 minutes (1)

Q28 (6 marks — Evaluate): Evaluate the contributions of each energy system 
throughout Luke's 1500m run completed in 4 minutes 25 seconds.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
- ATP-PC: explosive start/gun (0-10s), PC breaks down to ATP, quick recovery,
  minimal overall contribution given duration
- Lactate: surges and kicks (10s-3min bursts), glucose broken down anaerobically,
  lactate produced, used when intensity increases
- Aerobic: predominant system (majority of 4min 25s), uses O2,
  glucose/glycogen → Krebs → ETC → 36-38 ATP, sustainable
- All three contribute but aerobic dominates
- Impact: Luke must manage lactate accumulation, pacing crucial

## SECTION F — Interrelationships

Q29 (8 marks — Analyse): Analyse how the muscular system and skeletal system 
work together at the hip, knee and ankle to allow Sammy's right leg to move 
from Position A to Position B to strike the football.
Levels L1-L3 (7-8 = sustained clear interrelationships throughout). 
Indicative content:
- Hip flexion: iliopsoas/rectus femoris contract concentrically (agonist),
  gluteus maximus/hamstrings lengthen eccentrically (antagonist),
  ball and socket joint allows flexion, femur moves forward
- Knee extension: quadriceps contract concentrically (agonist),
  hamstrings relax as antagonist, hinge joint allows extension,
  tibia/fibula moves forward
- Ankle plantarflexion: gastrocnemius/soleus contract concentrically,
  tibialis anterior relaxes (antagonist), hinge joint → plantarflexion,
  tarsals/metatarsals push into ball
- Bones act as levers at each joint: muscle pulls on bone at insertion,
  joint acts as fulcrum
- Must link muscular action TO skeletal structure throughout
- Synergists stabilise at each joint

Q30 (8 marks — Analyse): Analyse how the muscular and cardiovascular systems 
respond to support Callum during the warm up and into a hockey match.
Levels L1-L3. Indicative content:
Muscular responses:
- Increased blood supply to muscle
- Increased temperature → increased pliability/elasticity
- Decreased muscle viscosity → faster/stronger contractions
- Increased speed and strength of contraction
- Reduced risk of injury (muscle strain/pulls)
Cardiovascular responses:
- Heart rate increases, stroke volume increases, cardiac output increases
- Blood pressure increases to pump blood faster
- Blood temperature increases → reduced viscosity → blood moves faster
- Vascular shunt mechanism activated → blood to working muscles
- Vasodilation at working muscles
Impact on performance:
- More oxygenated blood to muscles, CO2 removed
- Can work at higher intensity from start of match
- Fatigue delayed, can sustain performance throughout
