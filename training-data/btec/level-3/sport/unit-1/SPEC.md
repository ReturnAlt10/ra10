# BTEC Level 3 Sport Unit 1 — Anatomy and Physiology
# Pearson External Unit — 80 marks, 2 hours, 6 sections A-F

## Sections
A — The Skeletal System
  - Bones of the skeleton: cranium, clavicle, sternum, ribs, humerus, radius, ulna,
    carpals, metacarpals, phalanges, vertebrae (cervical/thoracic/lumbar/sacrum/coccyx),
    pelvis, femur, patella, tibia, fibula, tarsals, metatarsals
  - Types of bone: long, short, flat, irregular, sesamoid
  - Functions: support, protection, attachment for muscle, blood production,
    storage of minerals, leverage, weight-bearing, reduction of friction
  - Types of joint: pivot, hinge, ball and socket, condyloid, gliding, saddle
  - Cartilaginous joints: vertebrae, ribs/sternum, pubic symphysis
  - Types of cartilage: hyaline, fibrocartilage, elastic
  - Ligaments: connect bone to bone, stability, prevent unwanted movement
  - Range of movement: flexion, extension, abduction, adduction, rotation, circumduction,
    plantarflexion, dorsiflexion, pronation, supination

B — The Muscular System
  - Major muscles: trapezius, deltoid (anterior/posterior), pectoralis major,
    biceps, triceps, latissimus dorsi, erector spinae, rectus abdominis,
    external obliques, gluteus maximus, hip flexors (iliopsoas), quadriceps,
    hamstrings, gastrocnemius, soleus, tibialis anterior
  - Muscle fibre types:
    Type I (slow twitch): fatigue resistant, aerobic, small, red, high myoglobin,
    high mitochondria, high capillary density, low force, slow contraction
    Type IIa (fast oxidative glycolytic): moderate fatigue resistance
    Type IIb/X (fast twitch): powerful, fatigue quickly, white, anaerobic
  - Muscle contractions: concentric (isotonic), eccentric (isotonic), isometric
  - Roles: agonist (prime mover), antagonist, synergist, fixator
  - Motor unit and all-or-none law
  - Muscle mechanics: levers (first, second, third class), mechanical advantage
  - Responses to exercise: increased temperature, pliability, viscosity decrease
  - Chronic adaptations: hypertrophy, increased myoglobin, increased mitochondria,
    increased glycogen storage, increased capillary density, increased pliability

C — The Respiratory System
  - Structures: nasal cavity, trachea, bronchi, bronchioles, alveoli,
    diaphragm, intercostal muscles (internal/external), pleural membranes
  - Inspiration at rest vs exercise (additional muscles: scalenes, SCM, pectoralis minor)
  - Expiration at rest (passive) vs exercise (internal intercostals contract)
  - Lung volumes: tidal volume, IRV, ERV, residual volume, vital capacity,
    total lung capacity, minute ventilation (RR x TV)
  - Neural and chemical control of breathing: medulla oblongata, chemoreceptors,
    detect CO2/pH changes → increase breathing rate
  - Responses to exercise: increased breathing rate, increased tidal volume,
    increased minute ventilation, increased O2 uptake, increased CO2 removal
  - Chronic adaptations: increased vital capacity, stronger respiratory muscles,
    increased diffusion rates, increased number/size of alveoli,
    increased tidal volume, increased IRV decrease, ERV decrease

D — The Cardiovascular System
  - Heart structure: right/left atrium, right/left ventricle, septum,
    aorta, pulmonary artery, pulmonary vein, vena cava (superior/inferior),
    tricuspid valve, bicuspid/mitral valve, aortic semi-lunar valve,
    pulmonary semi-lunar valve, SA node, AV node, bundle of His, Purkinje fibres
  - Blood vessels: arteries, arterioles, capillaries, venules, veins
  - Capillaries: connect arterioles/venules, gas exchange site
  - Cardiac output = Heart rate x Stroke volume
  - Starlings Law: increased venous return → increased stretch → increased SV
  - Venous return mechanisms: muscle pump, respiratory pump, 
    venoconstriction, pocket valves
  - Vascular shunt mechanism: vasoconstriction/vasodilation, redistribution
  - Thermoregulation: vasodilation near skin surface, heat loss
  - Components of blood: red blood cells (carry O2 via haemoglobin), 
    white blood cells, platelets, plasma
  - Responses to exercise: increased HR, increased SV, increased cardiac output,
    increased blood pressure, redistribution of blood flow, vasodilation
  - Chronic adaptations: cardiac hypertrophy (thicker left ventricle wall),
    increased stroke volume, decreased resting heart rate, capillarisation,
    increased blood volume, increased haemoglobin/red blood cells

E — Energy Systems
  - ATP: adenosine triphosphate, immediate energy currency
  - ATP-PC system (alactic/phosphocreatine):
    Duration: 0-10 seconds, explosive high intensity
    Fuel: phosphocreatine (PC) → creatine + phosphate + energy → resynthesise ATP
    Anaerobic, no lactate produced
    Recovery: 2-4 minutes full replenishment
    Examples: 100m sprint, high jump, shot put, weightlifting
  - Lactate/glycolytic system:
    Duration: 10 seconds to 3 minutes, medium-high intensity
    Fuel: glucose/glycogen broken down anaerobically via glycolysis
    Produces ATP + pyruvate → lactate (when O2 insufficient)
    Recovery: 30 minutes to 2 hours
    Examples: 400m, 800m, repeated sprints
  - Aerobic oxidative system:
    Duration: 3 minutes+, low-medium intensity, sustainable
    Three stages: aerobic glycolysis, Krebs cycle, electron transport chain
    Fuel: glucose, glycogen, fats, protein (last resort)
    Produces most ATP (36-38 per glucose molecule)
    Requires oxygen, produces CO2 and water
    Examples: marathon, long-distance cycling, team sport endurance
  - Energy continuum: all three systems contribute simultaneously,
    dominance depends on intensity and duration
  - EPOC (excess post-exercise oxygen consumption): 
    fast component (replenish PC, remove lactate), slow component
  - VO2 max: maximum oxygen uptake, indicator of aerobic fitness
  - Lactate threshold: point where lactate accumulates faster than removal

F — Interrelationships Between Body Systems
  - How muscular and skeletal work together: muscles attach at origin/insertion,
    bones act as levers, joints allow movement, agonist/antagonist pairs
  - Joint actions during sport movements: specify joint, bones, muscles,
    type of contraction, type of movement at each joint
  - How cardiovascular and respiratory work together during exercise:
    oxygen uptake at alveoli → blood → heart → working muscles
    CO2 removed from muscles → blood → heart → lungs → exhaled
  - Warm up effects on all systems: increased HR, temperature, pliability,
    reduced viscosity, increased enzyme activity
  - Altitude training: lower air pressure, less O2 → hypoxia → adaptations
  - Asthma: airways restricted, breathing difficult

## Assessment
- 80 marks total, 2 hours
- 6 sections A-F, each tested separately
- Mix of short (1-4 mark) and extended (6-8 mark) questions
- Always link to named athlete and specific sport context
- Impact on performance required for Level 3 marks on extended questions
