Question 1 · Crestwell Coffee Ltd (32 marks)

Crestwell Coffee Ltd runs 46 coffee shops in towns across the Midlands and the North of England. It competes with national chains and independent cafés by focusing on high-quality, ethically sourced coffee. Its target market is professionals aged 25-45 who buy coffee on the way to work.

The business has opened four new shops in the last year, but the price of coffee beans has risen by 38% over two years and the National Living Wage rose by almost 10% in April 2024. Crestwell increased the price of a standard latte from £3.20 to £3.40, but directors fear that further price rises would drive customers to cheaper rivals.

Crestwell's marketing director wants to launch a monthly subscription: for £25 a month, subscribers could collect one hot drink per day. She believes it would 'lock in' loyal customers and attract new ones before a national rival launches a similar scheme. The finance director is less convinced.

Question 2 · Northgate Logistics plc (34 marks)

Northgate Logistics plc stores and delivers goods for online retailers from five large warehouses in England. Warehouse staff pick and pack orders by hand. Pay is close to the National Living Wage and most staff are on flexible contracts.

Demand for Northgate's services has grown, but its retail customers have complained about late deliveries and picking errors. Two retailers moved their contracts to a rival last year, which uses robots to pick orders.

Northgate's board is considering investing £36m in automated picking systems. The operations director says that this would allow 600 fewer warehouse staff to be employed. The HR director has warned that staff morale is already low.

Question 3 · Halden Furniture Ltd (34 marks)

Halden Furniture Ltd makes high-quality sofas to order in its factory in Lancashire. Customers choose the size, fabric and colour, and pay a 30% deposit when ordering. The remaining 70% is paid on delivery.

Orders have grown strongly, but the factory is running at 96% capacity utilisation and the average time between order and delivery has risen from 7 to 10 weeks. Rivals deliver in about 6 weeks. Halden holds large inventories of fabric 'just in case' customers choose them.

To reduce lead times, the directors are considering two options. Option 1: outsource the making of wooden sofa frames to a specialist supplier in Poland, freeing factory space for upholstery. Option 2: invest £1.2m in computer-controlled cutting machines, which would increase capacity by 25%.
