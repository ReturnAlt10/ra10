Case study · Apex Arena Ltd

Background

Jordan Reid founded Apex Arena Ltd in 2019 at the age of 23. Apex runs esports tournaments - competitive video-gaming events - in a converted warehouse in Manchester that holds 2 000 spectators. Every event is also streamed online. Apex earns revenue from tickets, sponsorship and advertising on its streams.

Apex has grown quickly. Revenue rose from £0.4m in 2020 to £3.6m in 2024. Jordan makes almost every important decision himself, often very quickly. He says: 'In this industry, if you wait for a report, someone else has already done it.' Apex was the first UK organiser to use virtual-reality viewing for online audiences.

People

Apex has 24 full-time staff and about 150 event staff on zero-hours contracts, who work as stewards, technicians and caterers at events. Event staff turnover is high (see Appendix B). Several full-time managers have left, complaining that Jordan does not let them make decisions.

The Northern Showdown

Apex is planning its biggest event yet, the 'Northern Showdown'. Jordan must decide whether to hold it in person in a hired 10 000-seat arena or as an online-only event. The forecasts are shown in Appendix A.

A new arena

Jordan wants to build Apex's own 5 000-seat arena at a cost of £3m. He is considering two ways of financing it.

Option 1: venture capital. A venture capital firm would invest £3m in return for 35% of Apex's shares and two seats on the board.

Option 2: a £3m bank loan at 8% interest a year, repayable over 10 years and secured on Apex's assets.
