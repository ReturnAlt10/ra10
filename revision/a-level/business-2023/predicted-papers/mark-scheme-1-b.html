Question 19 [9 marks]

Indicative content
  • Retained profit has no interest or repayment, so cash flow and profit for the year are not reduced by finance costs.
  • No dilution of ownership - existing shareholders keep control, unlike equity crowd funding where many new small shareholders join.
  • No need to publish plans publicly - crowd funding campaigns reveal strategy to competitors.
  • Quick and certain - crowd funding may fail to reach its target, and platform fees (typically 5-8%) add cost.
  • BUT retained profit may be insufficient for large projects; using it means lower dividends, which may upset shareholders.
  • Crowd funding can also act as marketing and market research (proof of demand) - a reason the business might prefer it in some cases.

Question 22 [25 marks]

'The product life cycle is of little value to a business with a large portfolio of products.' Do you agree? Justify your view.

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • With many products the Boston Matrix (market share and growth) is more useful for managing the whole portfolio and cash.
  • Life cycles are only clear in hindsight - predicting when a product will decline is hard, so decisions based on it may be wrong.
  • Large businesses use sales and profit data per product rather than a theoretical model.
Counter-arguments
  • Helps plan extension strategies and the timing of new product launches so that products are at different stages - balancing cash flow.
  • Guides marketing mix decisions at each stage (e.g. promotion heavy at launch, price cuts in decline).
  • Combines with the Boston Matrix - market growth reflects life cycle stage, so they are complementary.
  • Particularly valuable where life cycles are short (fashion, tech) and the portfolio must be renewed quickly.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

The model is valuable as a planning and portfolio-balancing tool when used with data and the Boston Matrix; its value is limited when used to predict precise timings.

Question 23 [25 marks]

To what extent is employee engagement more important than investment in technology in improving labour productivity?

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Engaged staff work harder, suggest improvements (kaizen) and take fewer days off - productivity rises without large capital spending.
  • Technology only raises productivity if staff are trained and willing to use it; disengaged workers resist change.
  • In services (care, hospitality) output depends heavily on people.
Counter-arguments
  • Automation and robotics can multiply output per worker many times (e.g. warehouses, car plants) - engagement alone cannot match this.
  • Capital intensive processes give consistent quality and speed.
  • Technology may itself raise engagement by removing boring repetitive tasks.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

They are complementary: technology sets the potential productivity, engagement determines how much of it is achieved; which matters more depends on the industry and the current level of automation.

Question 24 [25 marks]

Is a takeover of a local business always a better way to enter an overseas market than forming a joint venture? Justify your view.

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Full control of strategy, branding and profits; quick access to the local business's customers, distribution and knowledge.
  • No need to share profits or negotiate with a partner; avoids partner conflict.
  • Can achieve synergies and economies of scale faster.
Counter-arguments
  • Expensive - high premium paid; risk of overpaying and paying with debt (higher gearing).
  • Culture clash and integration problems are a major reason takeovers fail.
  • Joint ventures share risk and investment, give local knowledge, and may be required by law in some countries.
  • JVs are easier to exit if the market fails.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

Depends on the attractiveness and risk of the market, the finance available, legal restrictions and how much control matters - 'always' is unlikely to be true.

Question 25 [25 marks]

To what extent can resistance to strategic change always be overcome by education and communication?

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Much resistance comes from misunderstanding and lack of information (Kotter & Schlesinger) - explaining reasons builds trust.
  • Low cost, preserves relationships and supports lasting acceptance.
  • Good communication is the foundation for other methods (participation, negotiation).
Counter-arguments
  • Self-interest: if employees will lose out (pay, jobs), understanding the reasons will not remove resistance - negotiation may be needed.
  • Low tolerance for change and different assessments of the situation need participation/support.
  • Time - in a crisis, coercion may be the only practical method.
  • Depends on culture, trust in leaders, trade union involvement and scale of change.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

Education and communication are usually necessary but rarely sufficient; the best method depends on the cause of resistance and the urgency of the change.
