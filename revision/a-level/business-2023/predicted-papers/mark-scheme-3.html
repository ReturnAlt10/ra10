Using Appendix A, should Apex Arena Ltd hold the Northern Showdown in person or online only? Use quantitative and qualitative information and calculations to support your answer.

Supporting calculations
  • In person: EV = (0.6 x 900 000) + (0.4 x 500 000) = £740 000; net gain = £290 000
  • Online: EV = (0.5 x 420 000) + (0.5 x 260 000) = £340 000; net gain = £220 000
  • Worst cases: in person £500 000 - £450 000 = £50 000; online £260 000 - £120 000 = £140 000
Demands of the question
  • calculate expected values and net gains
  • analyse qualitative factors
  • make a judgement
Indicative content
  • in person: expected value £740 000, net gain £290 000 - higher than online (£220 000)
  • builds brand and sponsorship value; live atmosphere differentiates Apex
  • even with low attendance the in-person event earns £50 000 profit
  • online: much lower cost (£120 000) and risk; the worst outcome still gives £140 000 profit - more than the in-person worst case (£50 000)
  • probabilities are estimates - Apex has never filled a 10 000-seat arena
  • event staff turnover (70%) and falling satisfaction risk a poor live experience
Arguments for
  • in person: expected value £740 000, net gain £290 000 - higher than online (£220 000)
  • builds brand and sponsorship value; live atmosphere differentiates Apex
  • even with low attendance the in-person event earns £50 000 profit
Arguments against
  • online: much lower cost (£120 000) and risk; the worst outcome still gives £140 000 profit - more than the in-person worst case (£50 000)
  • probabilities are estimates - Apex has never filled a 10 000-seat arena
  • event staff turnover (70%) and falling satisfaction risk a poor live experience
Evaluation / judgement
  • the decision depends on Jordan's attitude to risk and the reliability of the forecasts
  • the difference in net gain (£70 000) may not justify nearly four times the cost
Examiner guidance
  • Highest level without a relevant calculation used in argument = Level 3.

  • analyse both sources of finance in context
  • make a justified recommendation
Indicative content
  • Option 1: no interest or repayments, protecting cash flow; VC expertise and contacts; shares risk in a volatile industry
  • Option 2: Jordan keeps 100% ownership and control; interest is predictable
  • Option 1: Jordan loses 35% ownership and faces board challenges to his autocratic style; VCs expect high returns and an exit
  • Option 2: interest of £240 000 a year is 44% of current operating profit; gearing rises from 18% to about 65%; assets at risk if revenue falls

To what extent is it more important for businesses in fast-changing markets to be flexible than to plan?

Demands of the question
  • understand strategic planning, emergent strategy and flexible organisations
  • analyse the value of flexibility
  • analyse the value of planning
  • judge
Indicative content
  • fast change makes long-term plans quickly outdated - emergent strategy allows responses
  • flexible structures (organic, delayered) and contracts allow quick adaptation
  • being first to market can give competitive advantage (e.g. Apex's VR viewing)
  • planning sets direction and coordinates resources; it helps avoid strategic drift
  • contingency planning prepares for crises
  • investors and lenders require plans
  • flexibility without direction leads to wasted resources
Arguments for
  • fast change makes long-term plans quickly outdated - emergent strategy allows responses
  • flexible structures (organic, delayered) and contracts allow quick adaptation
  • being first to market can give competitive advantage (e.g. Apex's VR viewing)
Arguments against
  • planning sets direction and coordinates resources; it helps avoid strategic drift
  • contingency planning prepares for crises
  • investors and lenders require plans
  • flexibility without direction leads to wasted resources
Evaluation / judgement
  • the two are complementary - a flexible plan with regular review
  • depends on how fast the market changes and the size of the business
