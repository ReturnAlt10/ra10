Question 18 [9 marks]

Indicative content
  • Retrenchment = reducing the scale of operations (closing sites, cutting product lines, selling divisions).
  • Falling demand (recession, changing tastes) leaves spare capacity - closing sites raises capacity utilisation and lowers unit costs.
  • Diseconomies of scale - coordination and communication problems; cutting layers improves control and speed.
  • Refocus on core competences - selling unrelated divisions frees cash and management time.
  • Improve cash flow and reduce gearing by selling assets in a financial crisis; may satisfy shareholders seeking higher ROCE.
  • BUT costs of redundancy, damage to morale and reputation, and loss of capacity if demand recovers.

Question 21 [25 marks]

To what extent is a low-cost positioning strategy more appropriate than differentiation for a business operating in a market where buyers have a lot of power?

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Powerful buyers (e.g. supermarkets buying from manufacturers) push prices down - only low-cost producers keep acceptable margins.
  • Standardised products with little to distinguish them give buyers easy alternatives - competing on cost is realistic.
  • Economies of scale and lean production allow survival at low prices.
Counter-arguments
  • Differentiation reduces buyer power by making the product hard to replace (strong brand, patents, unique features) - consumers demand it, so retailers must stock it.
  • Only one business can be the lowest-cost producer - others are squeezed.
  • Focus strategies (niche) can avoid powerful mass-market buyers by using direct-to-consumer channels.
  • Depends on the business's resources and core competences.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

Low cost can be necessary when products are commodities and buyers are concentrated, but differentiation is often the better long-term response because it changes the balance of power.

Question 22 [25 marks]

To what extent does the successful implementation of a strategy depend on effective communication?

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Employees must understand the strategy and their role in it; poor communication causes confusion, rumours and resistance.
  • Two-way communication brings feedback that helps managers adjust implementation.
  • Communication with external stakeholders (investors, suppliers) maintains support and finance.
Counter-arguments
  • Leadership, resources (finance, skills), organisational structure and culture are also critical.
  • Network analysis/project planning needed to coordinate activities on time.
  • A poor strategy will fail however well it is communicated; external change can derail implementation.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

Communication is a necessary condition but rarely sufficient; its importance increases with the scale of change and the number of people affected.

Question 23 [25 marks]

'Cash flow is more important than profit for all businesses.' Do you agree? Justify your view.

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Businesses fail because they run out of cash, not because they make a loss in one year - profitable businesses can fail through overtrading.
  • Cash pays wages and suppliers; lack of cash damages supplier relationships and credit terms.
  • Start-ups and fast-growing firms face long gaps between paying costs and receiving cash.
Counter-arguments
  • Long-term survival needs profit - losses eventually drain cash; investors and lenders judge businesses by profitability (ROCE).
  • Large, established businesses with overdraft facilities can manage short-term cash shortages.
  • Profit funds investment (retained profit) and is the main objective of shareholders.
  • Non-profits care about surpluses/break-even rather than profit.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

In the short run and for small or rapidly growing firms cash is usually more critical; in the long run profit is essential - the answer depends on time scale and the business's situation.

Question 24 [25 marks]

'Social media has made traditional forms of promotion unnecessary.' Do you agree? Justify your view.

Arguments supporting the statement / view
  • Social media is cheap, targeted (data on users) and measurable; viral marketing can reach millions at low cost.
  • Two-way engagement builds relationships and brand loyalty; influencers reach younger audiences who watch little TV.
  • Small businesses can compete with large ones.
Counter-arguments
  • Older audiences and some B2B customers are reached better by TV, print, trade fairs and sales teams.
  • Social media is crowded - organic reach is falling and paid ads are increasingly expensive.
  • Negative viral content can damage brands quickly; less control over message.
  • Integrated campaigns (TV + online) often work best; mass-market brands still use TV to build awareness.
Evaluation - what a top-level judgement looks like

Social media has reduced reliance on traditional promotion but has not made it unnecessary - the right mix depends on the target market, product and budget.
