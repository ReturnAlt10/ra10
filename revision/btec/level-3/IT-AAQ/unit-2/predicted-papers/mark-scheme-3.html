Mark Scheme — Predicted Paper 3

BTEC Level 3 AAQ IT Unit 2: Cyber Security & Incident Management • Total 90 marks

General marking guidance

  • Apply marks positively; reward what the student has shown.
  • Accept incorrect/phonetic spelling if the term is recognisable.
  • Accept any other appropriate/alternative response unless stated otherwise.
  • For 9-mark “Evaluate” questions, use the Levels-Based Mark Scheme at the end.

Question 1

1(a) Why unseparated guest Wi-Fi is a risk. (4 marks)

  • Guest devices share the network with internal systems (1)
  • guests could access/scan internal servers holding patient records (1)
  • malware on a guest device could spread to internal systems (1)
  • confidential patient data could be exposed or stolen (1)

1(b) How a USB security key works. (4 marks)

  • The USB key contains a cryptographic code/certificate (1)
  • when plugged in, the system reads/checks the code (1)
  • to verify the identity of the user (1)
  • providing a second “possession” factor alongside the password (1)

1(c)(i) Device-based security for a lost/stolen tablet. (4 marks)

  • Remote wipe/lock — erase data remotely when reported lost (2)
  • Full-disk encryption — data unreadable without the key (2)
  • Timed lockout/screen lock — prevents access without credentials (2)
  • plus linked expansion of how it protects the data (2)

1(c)(ii) Technology to locate a lost device. (1 mark)

  • GPS / Global Positioning System (or “phone home”/device tracking software) (1)

1(d) Impact of a data breach under GDPR. (9 marks)

Indicative content:

  • Legal/financial: fines up to £17.5m or 4% of annual turnover; notification to the ICO within 72 hours; compensation claims from affected patients.
  • Reputational: loss of patient trust; negative reviews; reduced reputation.
  • Operational: disruption to services; cost of remedial action and investigation.
  • Judgement: sensitive health data is a special category so consequences are especially severe.

Question 2

2(a) How to change the network to reduce risk. (6 marks)

  • Use a second switch or VLANs to segment the network (1)
  • place guest Wi-Fi on a separate segment (1)
  • so guest traffic only reaches the internet (1)
  • and cannot reach internal servers/staff systems (1)
  • apply access controls/firewall rules between segments (1)
  • reducing the attack surface for patient data (1)

2(b)(i) One vulnerability of a WAP. (2 marks)

  • Wi-Fi radio signals can pass outside the building (1)
  • so the network can be accessed from public areas (1)

Accept: default credentials, Evil Twin attack, wireless sniffing.

2(b)(ii) One way to secure a WLAN. (4 marks)

  • Use WPA2/WPA3 encryption (1)
  • so data transmitted over Wi-Fi is encrypted (1)
  • change default passwords/SSID (1)
  • use MAC filtering/block and allow lists (1)

2(c) Evaluate MAC address filtering. (9 marks)

Indicative content:

  • Staff: set list once; only update for new devices; blocks unlisted devices.
  • Guests: need to add each new device; time/resource overhead; limited MAC list size; inconvenient for patients.
  • General: MAC addresses can be spoofed so only medium security.

2(d) VPN function and drawback. (4 marks)

  • Establishes an encrypted tunnel over the internet (1)
  • protects data in transit from interception (1)
  • reduces risk of man-in-the-middle attack (1)
  • Drawback: cost of running the server, or slower speed (1)

Question 3

3(a) Two rules in an internet/email use policy. (2 marks)

  • No inappropriate/offensive/illegal material (1)
  • No downloading of executable files / no visiting dangerous websites (1)

Accept: no sending confidential information without authorisation, no upload of copyrighted material.

3(b) Staff responsibilities and training. (6 marks)

  • Complete required security training (1)
  • follow security procedures and report problems (1)
  • respond to/report suspicious activity (1)
  • maintain security in own workspace/behaviour (1)
  • Training: flexible, range of resources/learning styles (1)
  • ongoing for reinforcement and new threats, with progress tracking (1)

3(c) Procedures in an incident response policy. (6 marks)

  • Procedures for initial decisions/triage/escalation (2)
  • main response: analysis, containment, mitigation, recovery (2)
  • reviewing, reporting and closing the incident (2)

3(d) Disaster recovery purpose and triage plans. (6 marks)

  • Purpose: define how to recover IT/operations after a major incident (2)
  • Triage activates: cyber security incident response plan (1)
  • business continuity plan (1)
  • disaster recovery plan (1)
  • plus linked expansion (1)

Question 4

4(a) Isolating a device to preserve evidence. (4 marks)

  • Isolate from network/communications (1)
  • disable wireless / use airplane mode (1)
  • use a Faraday bag/cage to block signals (1)
  • retain current power state and photograph the screen (1)

4(b) Two challenges of live forensics. (4 marks)

  • Data is changing in situ, so evidence may be lost (2)
  • capturing data in active memory/RAM before power loss (2)

Accept: recovering corrupted data, capturing remote data, not losing temporary files.

4(c) Infrastructure reviewed during network forensics. (6 marks)

  • Firewalls and infrastructure devices (1)
  • switch (1), router (1), wireless access point (1)
  • client/server logs (1)
  • malware activity and alerts (1)

4(d) Two requirements for recording findings. (4 marks)

  • No action that could change data on the device (2)
  • Creation of a trail/record of all actions (chain of custody) (2)
  • Documentation of who is responsible for collection/decisions (2)

4(e) How the report prevents recurrence and improvements. (5 marks)

  • Report analyses the incident to identify errors/omissions in procedures (1)
  • identifies errors in preventative measures and delays in detection (1)
  • recommends remedial actions to prevent recurrence (1)
  • Improvements: IT policies (1) and security protection measures (physical, software, hardware, processes, training) (1)
