Mark Scheme — Predicted Paper 1

BTEC Level 3 AAQ IT Unit 2: Cyber Security & Incident Management • Total 90 marks

General marking guidance

  • Apply marks positively; reward what the student has shown.
  • Accept incorrect/phonetic spelling if the term is recognisable.
  • Accept any other appropriate/alternative response unless stated otherwise.
  • For 9-mark “Evaluate” questions, use the Levels-Based Mark Scheme at the end of this document.

Question 1

1(a)(i) State what is meant by phishing. (2 marks)

Award marks for a definition with linked expansion:

  • Fraudulent/emails that appear to come from a trusted source (1)
  • intended to trick the recipient into revealing personal information or opening a malicious link/attachment (1)

1(a)(ii) Explain one difference between phishing and spear phishing. (3 marks)

  • Phishing is mass/generic, sent to many people (1)
  • Spear phishing is targeted at a specific individual using personal research about them (1)
  • link to increased chance of success / harder to detect (1)

1(b)(i) Identify the type of malware. (1 mark)

  • Ransomware (1)

1(b)(ii) Explain one countermeasure to reduce spread. (3 marks)

  • Staff training/phishing awareness (1)
  • so staff recognise and do not open suspicious attachments (1)
  • reducing the chance of infection at source (1)

Accept: email filtering, antivirus/antimalware, network segmentation, backups.

1(c) Describe one security risk of BYOD. (4 marks)

  • Personal devices may not be patched/updated (1)
  • so they carry known vulnerabilities (1)
  • organisational data stored on a private device may be exposed if the device is lost/stolen (1)
  • and it is harder for the organisation to enforce its own security policy (1)

1(d) Evaluate the effectiveness of the backup strategy. (9 marks)

Indicative content:

  • Strengths: RAID 1 provides redundancy against single drive failure; daily backup provides some recoverability.
  • Weaknesses: single USB drive rotated daily means only one copy and it may be overwritten before ransomware is detected; RAID 1 is not a backup (mirrors corruption/deletion too); no off-site/cloud backup; no testing of restore process; USB drive kept onsite is vulnerable to theft/fire/ransomware.
  • Judgement: strategy is inadequate — does not follow the 3-2-1 rule, lacks off-site storage and restore testing.

Question 2

2(a) Explain the risk of a shared switch and the change needed. (6 marks)

  • Guest traffic shares hardware with the internal network (1)
  • guests could access/scan internal devices (1)
  • malware on guest devices could spread to internal systems (1)
  • use a second switch / VLANs to segment the network (1)
  • so guest traffic only reaches the internet (1)
  • and cannot reach internal servers/staff systems (1)

2(b)(i) State the purpose of an SSID. (1 mark)

  • To identify/name the wireless network (1)

2(b)(ii) Explain one consequence for guests if the SSID is hidden. (3 marks)

  • Guests cannot see the network listed on their device (1)
  • so they must ask staff for the network details (1)
  • which is inconvenient / slows the connection process (1)

2(c) Evaluate the use of MAC address filtering. (9 marks)

Indicative content:

  • Staff network: set list once; only update for new devices; blocks unlisted devices.
  • Guest network: need to add each new guest device; time/resource overhead; WAPs have limited MAC list sizes; inconvenient for new guests who must find their MAC address.
  • General: MAC addresses can be spoofed so only medium security; cost-benefit differs between staff and guest networks.

2(d) Describe how the VPN functions and one drawback. (4 marks)

  • Establishes an encrypted/protected connection over the internet (1)
  • data is encrypted so it cannot be read if intercepted (1)
  • reduces risk of man-in-the-middle attack (1)
  • Drawback: cost of running the server, or slower speed, or higher data usage (1)

Question 3

3(a) State the four stages of Plan-Do-Check-Act. (2 marks)

  • Plan, Do, Check, Act — award ½ per stage, any two correct stages for 1 mark (2 marks for all four)

3(b) Give two other sections of a password policy. (2 marks)

  • Frequency of password reuse / minimum password age (1)
  • Prevent password sharing / disallow personal names and birthdays (1)

Accept: lockout policy, deny lists of common passwords.

3(c) Role of DPO and importance of data protection policy. (6 marks)

  • DPO monitors compliance with GDPR/data protection principles (1)
  • advises on data protection obligations and staff training (1)
  • acts as contact point for the regulator and data subjects (1)
  • policy protects rights/privacy of data subjects (1)
  • ensures system security procedures are followed (1)
  • applies to external contractors/vendors who handle data (1)

3(d) Three contacts in an incident response policy. (6 marks)

  • Incident response team leader/provider — coordinates the response (2)
  • Senior management — authorises decisions and resources (2)
  • Legal / public relations / HR / insurance — manages legal, reputational and staff implications (2)

3(e) Purpose of disaster recovery policy and three triage plans. (6 marks)

  • Purpose: defines how the organisation recovers IT/operations after a major incident (2)
  • Triage activates: cyber security incident response plan (1)
  • business continuity plan (1)
  • disaster recovery plan (1)
  • plus linked expansion of purpose (1)

Question 4

4(a) Two actions to preserve a seized device. (2 marks)

  • Isolate from network/communications (1)
  • Retain current power state / photograph the screen (1)

Accept: use Faraday bag, document chain of custody, appropriate packaging.

4(b) Importance of chain of custody. (5 marks)

  • Records who has handled the evidence at every stage (1)
  • proves the evidence has not been altered/tampered with (1)
  • so evidence remains admissible in legal proceedings (1)
  • documents responsibility for collection/decisions (1)
  • creates an unbroken trail from seizure to court (1)

4(c) Why a hash is generated for a forensic copy. (4 marks)

  • A hash/checksum is a unique digital fingerprint of the data (1)
  • comparing hashes verifies the copy is identical to the original (1)
  • any change to the data produces a different hash (1)
  • so integrity of the evidence can be proven (1)

4(d) Three indicators of compromise. (8 marks)

  • Unusual inbound/outbound traffic or traffic from unusual geographical regions (2)
  • Unusual login attempts / unusual DNS requests (2)
  • Increase in file/data reads or requests / unusual port usage (2)
  • Suspicious changes to files or system data (2)

4(e) Report structure and improvements. (4 marks)

  • Structure: title, contents, introduction, headings/sub-headings, conclusions (2)
  • Improvements: improvements to IT policies; improvements to security protection measures (physical, software, hardware, processes, training) (2)
